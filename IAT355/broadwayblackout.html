How has the popularity of Broadway shows shifted across 1900-2020?

Broadway saw a surge in the volume of titles opened in the 1900s and achieved its all time peak during the 1920s, before meeting a sharp decline in the subsequent decades.

The surge in openings during the 1900s can be partly attributed to a wave of new theaters being built in the Broadway district, including the Republic, the Lyric, New Amsterdam and the Lew Fields.

After a resurgence in the 1980s, the numbers settled into a stable period until 2020, which brought the amount of titles opened to an unprecedented zero

The Golden Age of Broadway

The golden age of Broadway lines up with the post World War I economic boom known as the Roaring Twenties (Cunningham). Rising incomes and technological growth gave Americans more time and money for leisure, and Broadway was a direct beneficiary, with iconic productions like Chicago and the Great Gasby, cementing this district as a cultural centerpiece of the decade (Broadway Inbound).

The 1920s and Onwards

The sharp decline in openings following the 1920s peak aligns directly with the Great Depression, which spanned from 1929 to 1939. Widespread unemployment and collapsing household incomes left little room for leisure spending, cutting into both audience demand and the financial viability of producing new shows. The result was a decade-long contraction that effectively reversed Broadway's golden era growth.
